# Market: what's popping in AI Shorts (snapshot 2026-10-29)

HQ research for the Studio test. The radar refreshes this daily once built (see `../radar/`); HQ re-reads this file weekly. Numbers are what the sources report; third-party estimates are marked "est.".

## TL;DR
- **The live wave: AI animals living adult human life** (office, commute, overtime, rent, groceries). In China ("cyber furballs", Oct 26): an AI cat daily-life series ~450M views, a chubby-cat series ~190M; typical plots include a cat office worker who commutes, works overtime and buys snacks on the way home; #AICuteAnimals 2.64B views from 124k creators. The same lane in the West: capi.and.bara (2 recurring capybaras, wordless, <20 s), the "Me At Work" cat meme (Oct), FUNTASTIC YT (recurring kitten, ~600k subs, ~500M views, best month $9k), Bandar Apna Dost (recurring monkey, ~2.07B views, est. $4.25M/yr).
- **Proven but crowded: fruit/food micro-dramas.** Fruit Love Island did 300M+ views in ~2 weeks (March 2026). Copies in English, Spanish and Hindi still pull 1–5M per video in Oct. Backlash (misogyny, "slop"), TikTok removals.
- **Biggest AI Shorts in search:** football parodies of real players (478M, 71M) and **AI comedy ads** (43M, 36M, 36M). Real players = likeness risk; comedy ads = open format.
- **Breakouts are rare:** 7–12 a week across YouTube's AI topics (≥200k views from channels under 30k subs). Median breakout channel: 2.6k–6.3k subs. 2–4 a week come from channels under 3 months old.
- **Averages are brutal:** AI comic-style dramas average ~5.8k views per title (median ~2.9k). In China, 0.117% of AI dramas pass 100M. Cast, writing, look and speed decide; the tool doesn't.

## What the winners share
- **A recurring cast.** Fruit Love Island, capi.and.bara, Bandar Apna Dost, FUNTASTIC YT, the cyber-furball series. Viewers come back for the character, not the gag.
- **One situation, one reversal.** Slop TV's 1M-view Short is a single animal situation with a twist in its title ("won the fight, then lost everything").
- **Wordless or nearly wordless.** capi.and.bara uses no dialogue, only SFX; the 14M-view tiny-animal POV channel uses no narration. No language barrier, no voice drift between shots.
- **Short.** Gags run 15–35 s; episodic drama 60–90 s (Fruit Love Island: 2–4 min).
- **Series packaging.** 21% of tracked AI shorts are numbered or episodic; 11% end on a cliffhanger. YouTube launched Shorts Series (seasons/episodes) on Sept 23, 2026, for YPP creators only.
- **No faces of real people, no host on camera** (100% of BreakoutLens's tracked set).
- **Output pace:** FUNTASTIC YT 1–2/day at 1–2 h each; the tiny-animal channel ~2/day; AI comic-drama channels up to 3–4/day (and the lowest averages).

## Failures worth remembering
- **No Big Deal** (first "fully AI sitcom", 25-min pilot, Sept 11): 630 views in 2 days. Long AI sitcoms with no audience don't work.
- **Jan 2026 purge:** 16 channels (35M subs, 4.7B views) terminated or wiped. Super Cat League (AI animals, 4.2M subs) and CuentosFacianantes (episodes that differed only in names and plot fragments). Pattern: AI did script, voice, visuals and publishing with no human direction, at high volume.
- **AI comedian "Tiffany Sloan"** (IG, 375k followers in 3 weeks): accused of stealing comedians' jokes. Our jokes are written for our cast.
- **Glorb** (AI SpongeBob voices, 381M views): built on someone else's characters. Never us.

## Money facts
- **Shorts RPM:** US ~$0.33 per 1k (AIR, 2025 data); 2026 average ~$0.03–0.07 (fact-checker). One creator: ~9M qualified views ≈ $2,600 (~$0.29 per 1k).
- **Shorts pay floor from Feb 1, 2027:** Shorts revenue only in months with ≥10M qualified views per rolling 90 days; new applicants need 20M + 1k subs. Long-form revenue is unaffected.
- **Long-form:** animation channels average ~$7 RPM (older audit); general-audience entertainment ~$5–15; **made for kids $1–3 (50–80% less)**.
- **AI channels at scale:** 278 AI "slop" channels had 63B views and est. $117M/yr (Kapwing, 2025). FUNTASTIC YT's best month: $9k (NPR, 2025).

## Policy facts
- **July 16, 2026 rules (not monetized):** generic/template content that varies little between videos; content made to upset or manipulate (YouTube's example: an animal in distress that someone rescues); AI personas of real people talking finance, legal or health.
- **Allowed:** a recurring host, character or mascot; a fixed episode structure with new substance each time; consistent intros and editing style.
- **Made for kids:** YouTube lists "animated characters or cartoon figures" as one factor, but animation alone doesn't make a video made for kids; general-audience animation is fine. Topic, language, songs for children and stated audience decide. YouTube's systems can flag it too.
- **Disclosure:** realistic synthetic content needs the "altered or synthetic" label; clearly animated or unrealistic content generally doesn't. Per YouTube, labels don't change reach or monetization. Omni output carries a SynthID watermark.

## What we copy, what we avoid
- **Copy (the format):** length, number of beats, hook type, reversal ending, caption look, title pattern, posting time, series packaging. The radar updates these daily.
- **Never copy (the content):** characters, stories, footage, voices, brands. Our cast is ours.
- **Avoid:** babies and kids, real people, lookalikes of existing characters, real brands and logos, cheating/sexual tropes, animal distress or rescue, template clones with swapped nouns, trending music, personal photo-template trends.
